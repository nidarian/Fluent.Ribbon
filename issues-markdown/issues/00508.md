## batzen commented on 2018-02-03T17:12:12Z

That's the way WPF works.
To override the style for all your windows, without having to set it explicitly, you have to inherit from `RibbonWindow` and apply your custom default style to that class.

Example for the Showcase application, definition of style in `App.xaml`:
```xaml
<Style x:Key="{x:Type fluentTest:TestWindow}" 
        TargetType="{x:Type fluent:RibbonWindow}">
    <Setter Property="IsIconVisible"
            Value="False" />
</Style>
```

Does that answer your question?

## batzen commented on 2018-02-18T14:21:43Z

If you inherit from `RibbonWindow` you have to target your style at that subclass.
That's the way WPF resolves styles for windows and there is nothing i can do about that.

Your accent is not an accent in the way Fluent.Ribbon defines it.
You have to provide resources in your accent file, not styles.
Just have a look at one of the provided accents. Please not that the accents are generated at compile time, so they are not committed to git.

## batzen commented on 2018-02-18T14:53:36Z

Your MainWindow is inherited from RibbonWindow.
If you want to apply a style to all your windows just create a new class MyRibbonWindow which inherits from RibbonWindow and inherit all your other windows from MyRibbonWindow. That way it works the way you want it to work.

You can place whatever resources in your accent dictionary you like to place there, but i would keep them separated. And yes, the accent dictionaries only contain resources relevant for that one accent. So you should also keep accent resources and styles separated, but you don't have to.
